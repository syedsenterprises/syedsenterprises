Seasonal Variation and a Declining Trend in Hyderabad's Air Quality, 2015–2020: A Statistical Analysis of Daily CPCB AQI Data

Syed Shahed · St. Mary's College, Yousufguda, Hyderabad, Telangana, India · Published 25 November 2026 · Open access (CC-BY-4.0)

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24538115 · Zenodo record: zenodo.org/records/24538115

Download the PDF Read on Zenodo All research

Abstract

Hyderabad's air quality is something I live with every day. But most of what I had read about it was either a single day's headline number or a vague claim that pollution is rising. This paper answers, with real data: is Hyderabad's air actually getting worse, getting better, or staying the same — and which season is the worst? Using 2,006 days of daily CPCB AQI data (4 January 2015 to 1 July 2020), the paper finds that Hyderabad's air improved significantly: mean AQI fell from 143.42 in 2015 to 93.98 in 2019, a decline of about 13.1 points per year that is real and not a lockdown artefact. Winter is the worst season (mean AQI 131.24) and monsoon the best (85.52). PM2.5 is the main driver of AQI. But the air is still far from safe: mean PM2.5 is about 9 times the WHO 2021 guideline.

Key findings

Mean AQI fell from 143.42 (2015) to 93.98 (2019) — a decline of 13.1 points per year. The 2015–2019 trend alone is −12.96/year (p = 3.15e-44): a real improvement, not a lockdown artefact.

Winter is worst, monsoon is best. Winter mean AQI 131.24; post-monsoon 125.96; summer 108.55; monsoon 85.52. The winter–monsoon gap is 45.72 points (ANOVA F = 86.92, p < 0.001).

PM2.5 drives AQI. Correlation with AQI: PM2.5 r = 0.798, PM10 r = 0.677. AQI on PM2.5 alone explains 63.6% of the variance (R² = 0.636); adding PM10 raises it to 79.2%.

Still far from safe. Mean AQI 109.21 ("Moderate"). Mean PM2.5 47.04 µg/m³ is ~9× the WHO guideline (5 µg/m³); mean PM10 91.93 µg/m³ is ~6× the guideline (15 µg/m³).

Data and method

Daily city-level AQI from the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB), via the public AQI-Of-India dataset. Hyderabad subset: 2,006 days, 2015-01-04 to 2020-07-01; AQI present on 1,880 days. The analysis uses descriptive statistics, seasonal comparison (ANOVA and Kruskal-Wallis), correlation and regression. The data is not normally distributed (Shapiro-Wilk W = 0.8046, p < 0.001), so non-parametric tests are used where appropriate. The lockdown window (25 March–31 May) is compared 2019 vs 2020 separately: mean AQI 105.65 vs 71.75.

Why it matters

For a city of over 10 million people, "the air is getting better" is a meaningful, evidence-based statement — but "better" is not "good." This paper gives Hyderabad a baseline: the trend, the seasons, and the pollutant that matters most. Anyone working on the city's air — researchers, policymakers, or residents — can build on these numbers instead of starting from headlines.
